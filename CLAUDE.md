# Shaman film: engine rules

A serialized pixel-art film, drawn entirely by code. Every session working
here inherits these rules. Change them only when drew says so.

## Format (locked)
- Base resolution 320x180, upscaled x6 to 1920x1080 with nearest-neighbour, in FFmpeg only.
- 12 fps. Animation cycles run slower (sprites declare their own `fps`, usually 2 to 6).
- Palette: `engine/palette.py` only. Never write hex anywhere else. A muted base (ash,
  sky, cold, rust, moss), a night blue, and one ramp per region of the world
  (mauve uplands, tide coast, blood glass towns, fern interior). `ember` is
  reserved for rig lights, fire, and the capture.
- Every scene sets `backdrop.region` and `time` (`engine/regions.py`) so places
  look different as the journey moves. See `docs/style.md` for the full brief.
- No ordinary birds or bats (the Quiet took them); insects fill the air. Fantastical
  birds exist and are carnivorous predators, gryphon-like threats, never background
  life. Creatures of the Americas' folklore and myth share the world (see docs/style.md).
- No alpha blending and no anti-aliasing. Tones mix only by Bayer dithering (`engine/dither.py`).
  Fades, glows, captions and transitions all dither.
- Text: all story text is on-screen subtitles, no voice. Use `cards` (`engine/subtitles.py`):
  narration centred with no box, dialogue in a box with the speaker's name tag
  (`assets/speakers.yaml`). Font: `engine/textfont.py`, mixed case. Text types on at
  40 chars/s and holds for chars / 15 + 1 s. Episodes set `band: 40`, a black strip
  under the picture that holds all text, so it never covers the figures. Long cards
  split into pages by themselves. `captions` and the old 3x5 font are for titles only.
- Scripts come from drew in the scene format in `docs/script-sample.md`;
  `python tools/script2timeline.py <script> episodes/<ep>` writes the draft timeline.

- Action: fights are rare, short and brutal, and are cut, not animated long. Split a
  scene into `shots` (punch-ins with whole-number `zoom` + `focus`, a few frames each),
  then `fx` from `engine/fx.py`: speed lines, impact frames (flat colour, figures in
  silhouette), flashes, letterbox bars, vignette, plus camera `shake` and actor
  `tint`/`freeze`. `episodes/ep00_fight` is the first cut; `episodes/ep00_fights` has one
  scene per fight type (standoff, ambush, creature, chase, capture).

- Shots (drew, 2026-11-06): no scene is a figure sliding across one backdrop. Every
  scene is cut into a shot list. Scripts carry `SHOT: size · angle · subject · move · hold`
  lines; the vocabulary and what each does are in `docs/shots.md` and `engine/shotlist.py`
  (`shot: "close · low · auk · push-in"` on a scene, shot or panel). Tools: foreground
  props (`layer: front`), screen-pinned props (`layer: screen`), `backdrop.angle`
  (low/high/skyline), `panels` (manga pages), `plain` backdrops for inserts, the Survey
  `map` props, camera `focus_on`/`follow`, actor `scale`. `episodes/ep00_shots` shows each.
- Close and insert sprites (`auk_close`, `teal_close`, `rig_face`, `hands_crank`) and the
  rig and the gaunt are generated by `tools/art/*.py` (raster kit in `tools/art/raster.py`);
  edit the generator, re-run it, and review with `tools/lineup.py`. Hand-drawn sprites
  stay hand-drawn.
- Nothing stands like a statue: give idle actors `breathe`, walkers `bob`, and offset
  cycles with `phase`.

- Ambient supernatural scenery (drew, 2026-11-05): eyes in the dark, wanderlights,
  tall half-there presences, as background only, never explained. They are props
  (`eyes`, `wanderlight`, `presence` in `engine/props.py`); `episodes/ep00_ambient` shows them.
  Don't stack eyes with firefly `motes` weather: they read the same.

## Layout
- `engine/` the reusable engine. Most review effort goes here.
- `assets/sprites/*.yaml` ASCII sprites (format documented in `engine/sprites.py`).
  Draw facing right; actors flip. A sprite enters the library only after its
  contact sheet (`python tools/lineup.py <name>`) is approved.
- `episodes/<ep>/timeline.yaml` one episode as data: scenes with duration, backdrop,
  camera, weather, actors, lights, captions, transitions. Edit this, not prose.
- `out/` renders (gitignored).

## Loop
0. Draft the timeline from the script with `tools/script2timeline.py`, then hand-pass camera,
   paths, props and lights (the draft's `notes` say what each scene needs).
1. `python render.py episodes/<ep> --keys` for key frames + `out/<ep>/contact.png` (seconds).
   With cards, a key frame is also taken as each card finishes typing.
2. Review the contact sheet, adjust the timeline or sprites.
3. `python render.py episodes/<ep> --scene <id>` for one clip, then the full episode.
4. Tag each approved cut in git (`ep01-cut1`, ...).
